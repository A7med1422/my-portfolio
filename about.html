I got into AI through a specific frustration: watching capable models fail the moment they met a real user.

Most of what I build starts with vision — hands, faces, motion, objects — and ends somewhere a person can use it: a phone, a dashboard, an API. The interesting engineering lives in between, in the compromises between accuracy, speed, and how legible the result is to someone who doesn't care about architectures.

Echo Lite is the clearest example. Recognising a sign is a research problem; holding a conversation is a product problem. Solving the first without the second produces a demo. Doing both produces something a person keeps on their phone.

Alongside vision, I work on agentic and retrieval-grounded systems, and on the data side — forecasting, evaluation design, and turning model output into something a manager can act on in under a minute. The through line is the same: intelligence is only useful once it's usable.

Where I'm heading: deeper into on-device inference and agentic systems that combine perception with action, and toward accessibility work, where the difference a well-built model makes is immediate and personal.

HOW I WORK

Four rules I don't break

01

Evaluation before architecture

The metric decides what the system optimises for, so it gets chosen first — with the failure mode that actually costs something in mind, not the one that scores well.

02

Latency is a feature

An accurate answer that arrives too late is a failed answer. In real-time work I design the pipeline around the time budget and fit the model into it.

03

Output people can argue with

Confidence scores, visual explanations, cited sources, refusal paths. If a user can't check the system, they can only trust it blindly or ignore it entirely.

04

Finish the last mile

monitoring, fallbacks, deployment, and the small pieces of engineering that keep A notebook is not a product. I build the service and the client too, because most of the value appears in the part after the model.

The fastest way to judge an engineer is to read their work.
